The easiest way to derail a car-free Sokcho trip is to treat its two bus terminals as one place. Check the exact arrival and departure terminals on your tickets before choosing a hotel or saving a walking route. This is a desk-researched planning guide, not a report of a personally tested journey. Official transport references were checked on September 13, 2026.

## Express Terminal or Intercity Terminal?

The city lists Sokcho Express Bus Terminal at **3994 Donghae-daero**. It is the useful starting area for a beach-led visit. The Intercity Bus Terminal is a different arrival point; for that terminal, compare a market-first plan with a northern waterfront visit. Search the Korean names **속초고속버스터미널** and **속초시외버스터미널**, then match the pin to your ticket. [Sokcho city transport information](https://www.sokcho.go.kr/ct/tour/tour_guide/traffic)

| Your arrival | A practical first move | What needs a separate transfer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Express Terminal, small bag | Sokcho Beach and a meal | Market and Abai Village area |
| Intercity Terminal | Market-area lunch | Beach if you still want a coast stop |
| Either terminal, suitcase | Confirm luggage storage with your hotel | Sightseeing after the bag is stored |
| Afternoon arrival | Stay on the same side as your hotel or departure | Leave a mountain visit for another day |

These are itinerary choices, not guaranteed walking times. For city buses, use the [official route search](https://bis.sokcho.go.kr/search?type=route) and check the direction as well as the number. A stop across the road may serve the reverse journey.

## A Coast-and-Market Day

From the Express Terminal, begin with the beach while your bag is light. Transfer to the Tourist and Fishery Market area for a meal, then decide whether to add Abai Village. Do not buy all your takeaway food at the beginning: you will have to carry it through the rest of the walk, and some dishes are better eaten promptly.

If the small Gaetbae crossing is part of the experience you want, allow time for its operation and queue. A land route using a bridge is not the same distance as the short water crossing; search a new walking route rather than assuming it is a quick substitute. The city tourism portal identifies these as distinct attractions, not one continuous beach promenade. [Sokcho tourism](https://www.sokcho.go.kr/ct/tour)

For a same-day return, end the sightseeing near the bag you need to collect. Check the terminal again before ordering a taxi. Keeping the ticket's Korean terminal name ready is more useful than saying only “Sokcho bus station.”

## When Seoraksan Fits

Seoraksan requires a journey inland and time on site. Decide what you mean by visiting: a Sogongwon entrance-area walk, a cable-car ride and a longer hike have different time demands. The city identifies buses 7 and 7-1 for the Seoraksan direction. Look up the exact service and return direction in the [official timetable](https://bis.sokcho.go.kr/schedule).

**A depot departure time is not the last return bus from Sogongwon.** Do not copy the final time on a timetable without checking its origin. If the mountain is your priority, go early and make the market your later meal stop. If you reach Sokcho around lunchtime, a beach-and-market day is the more forgiving choice. Adding a cable-car queue and a full beach afternoon to a short mountain visit leaves little recovery time if transport slips.

## Where to Stay for One Night

A beach-area base suits an arrival walk and an evening by the sea, but does not remove the next day's transfer to the mountain. A mountain-led trip needs a hotel with a useful bus-stop connection, not simply “Seorak” in its name. Ask whether the property will hold luggage after checkout; otherwise the return from the park can involve a detour before the intercity bus home.

Do not calculate taxi savings using a made-up flat fare. Compare a live quote for the whole group with the bus cost for that same journey. In heavy rain, shorten exposed waterfront walking, but do not assume every market lane is covered. Choose one priority for the day, then protect the transport that gets you home.
