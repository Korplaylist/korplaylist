Choose **one side of Jeju** before planning a car-free stay. A bus connecting distant districts does not make several coastal attractions a quick day trip. Waiting and walking from the stop are part of the journey.

## Coastal Routes Are Not One Airport Shuttle

The official system lists 201 on the eastern corridor between Jeju Bus Terminal, Seongsan Ilchulbong and Seogwipo Bus Terminal; 202 serves the western corridor via Gosan. Search the connection from the airport separately. An express service also need not stop at every local sightseeing stop. [Official Jeju route schedules](https://bus.jeju.go.kr/publicTrafficInformation/generalBusSchedule)

Work backwards: identify the attraction's stop, the bus that serves it, then the airport connection. Use the [current mobile timetable](https://bus.jeju.go.kr/mobile/schedule/listSchedule), not a saved years-old map, when route revisions are announced.

## Choose a Hotel by Its Final Approach

A Jeju City base reduces hotel changes but can create long return trips. A coastal base suits a slower stay in one district, while changing hotels can continue the route but adds luggage handling. Compare these trade-offs before booking.

Search the walking route from the actual bus stop to the hotel entrance. Being near a beach does not establish a comfortable pavement for a suitcase, especially after dark. Ask the property about access when the map does not resolve the last stretch.

## Payment and the Return Journey

Korea Tourism Organization reports cashless operation on Jeju city buses. Prepare a supported transport card and sufficient balance rather than relying on cash. [KTO's Jeju bus guide](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=220897)

Save the return route when researching the outward one. One extra attraction adds not just visiting time but another wait. On departure day, protect the airport connection first; an immediately available taxi is not a guaranteed rescue for a missed last bus.
