Haedong Yonggungsa is best planned as an eastern Busan outing, not as a quick stop between attractions near Busan Station. The temple's rocky coastal setting is the main reason to go, but reaching it, walking the stairs and finding the return bus all need time. Combine it with a flexible Haeundae-area afternoon rather than a tightly timed cross-city checklist.

For an independent first visit, use public transport if you are comfortable checking a bus stop and walking the last section. Consider a taxi for the final leg when several people are sharing or walking stamina is limited. A taxi can reduce the approach, but it does not remove steps inside the temple grounds.

## Locate the right temple before planning transport

Save Haedong Yonggungsa, 해동용궁사, at 86 Yonggung-gil in Gijang. It is on Busan's northeastern coast. The similarly named Songdo Yonggung Suspension Bridge is a different attraction on the other side of the city. Searching only "Yonggung" can therefore lead to the wrong outing.

The [official Visit Busan listing](https://www.visitbusan.net/index.do?lang_cd=en&menuCd=DOM_000000301001001000&uc_seq=261) lists free admission and hours of 04:30 to 19:20, with last entry at 18:50. Treat these as the published reference, then recheck before an early or late visit. Access restrictions, ceremonies and weather may affect what is available.

Do not assume an early opening means public transport is already convenient. A sunrise visit requires a separate transport plan and enough daylight or appropriate lighting to negotiate steps safely. A normal morning visit is usually easier to fit into an independent travel day.

## Haeundae or Osiria: which approach makes sense?

Visit Busan identifies an approach from Haeundae Station Exit 7 using buses including 181, and another from Osiria Station on the Donghae Line using bus 139. Check a live route planner for the current stop, direction and next departure before boarding. These are route references, not a fixed departure schedule.

If you are staying around Haeundae, starting there avoids an unnecessary train detour. If your journey already follows the Donghae Line, Osiria may fit better. Compare the complete door-to-door journey, including the walk from your hotel and the wait at the transfer, rather than comparing only time spent on a moving train.

Save the temple bus stop and your return destination in Korean as well as English. The stop used for the return direction may be across a road or in a different position. Check it while arriving, before you are tired and watching a departure countdown.

Carry a topped-up local transit card if that is how you are traveling around Busan. Do not count on a foreign bank card being interchangeable with a local transit card at every bus reader. Keep your phone charged and enough mobile data for route updates. A screenshot is useful for names, but it cannot show a changed bus arrival time.

## The visit includes stairs in both directions

The [Korea Tourism Organization's temple feature](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?dataSetId=76&menuSn=508&vcontsId=195451) highlights the stair approach. This is a reason to consider the return climb before descending, not a reason to count every step while looking at your phone.

Wear shoes with grip and use handrails where available. Keep the middle of narrow steps clear for people moving in the opposite direction. Stop at a wider landing to take a photograph rather than stopping suddenly on a staircase.

A stroller is not a good substitute for checking the route. Travelers using wheelchairs or other mobility aids should contact the site about current accessible areas and limitations before traveling. Do not assume that taxi access to the entrance establishes step-free access to the coastal viewpoints.

## A slow route through the coastal grounds

Allow about 60 to 90 minutes at the temple for the approach, open visitor areas, a quiet pause and the return climb. Add transport and waiting separately. This is our sightseeing estimate; crowds or reduced mobility can make it longer.

Begin by orienting yourself near the entrance, then follow the permitted route toward the coastal grounds. Let worshippers pass and respect any restrictions around prayer halls. A temple visit can be worthwhile without entering every interior or reaching every viewpoint.

The coast is scenery to observe from maintained visitor areas. Do not cross a barrier, climb wet rocks or move closer to breaking waves to imitate a photograph. In strong wind or rain, reduce the coastal portion and consider postponing the outing altogether.

Keep your voice low, do not stage photographs in front of people praying, and ask before taking close-up pictures of individuals. Food and shopping can wait until you reach an appropriate area outside the quiet visiting spaces.

## Return transport is part of the plan

Before leaving the temple grounds, check your onward route again. A bus that looked convenient on the morning journey may involve a longer wait later. Keep enough flexibility to have a drink or rest without missing a fixed evening commitment.

If choosing a taxi, use a clearly identified pickup location that vehicles can reach legally. Do not expect an immediate available car at a busy or isolated moment. Confirm your destination before departing, particularly if your hotel name is similar to another property's name.

A solo traveler may prefer the bus for cost control. A family sharing a taxi may value fewer transfers more than the lowest individual fare. Compare the current quoted fare with your actual group size; this guide does not give a guaranteed taxi price because traffic and pickup location affect it.

Avoid using the temple as a last-minute stop before a departing KTX train. The cross-city journey and local transfer can create a larger risk than the map's straight-line distance suggests. On departure day, choose something closer to your station unless you have a substantial buffer.

## Build an eastern Busan half-day

A sensible sequence is hotel to Haeundae or Osiria, onward transport to the temple, a 60-to-90-minute visit, then lunch and one flexible coastal stop. Allow roughly half a day overall rather than promising a two-hour hotel-to-hotel excursion. The exact duration depends heavily on where you are staying.

If you have a timed beach-train or Sky Capsule booking, leave a generous gap and check the boarding station. Read our [Blueline Park comparison](/en/travel/busan/busan-blueline-beach-train-vs-sky-capsule/) before assuming those services are interchangeable. Do not purchase a tight slot merely because both activities are in eastern Busan.

For accommodation decisions, the [Busan first-trip stay-area guide](/en/travel/busan/busan-stay-area-first-trip/) explains why your base changes the shape of each day. Haeundae-area sightseeing belongs together more naturally than a repeated commute to and from Nampo.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Haedong Yonggungsa free?

The official tourism listing gives free admission. Transport, parking, food and optional purchases are separate expenses.

### Can I get there entirely by subway?

The official routes include an onward bus connection from Haeundae or Osiria. Check the last leg rather than assuming the station exit is at the temple gate.

### Should I visit at sunrise?

Only with transport and safe access planned in advance. An ordinary morning visit is easier if you rely on public transport and do not know the steps.
