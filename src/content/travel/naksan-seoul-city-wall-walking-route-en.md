The Naksan section of Seoul City Wall is a useful first city walk when you want hillside views without committing to a mountain summit. Start near Hyehwamun, follow the wall toward Naksan Park, and finish by Heunginjimun Gate and Dongdaemun Station. This gives the outing a clear direction and avoids returning uphill simply to reach your starting station.

It is still a walk with slopes and steps. Travelers with rolling luggage, painful knees or a tight appointment should not interpret "easy city walk" as flat, step-free pavement. Allow around two hours with photographs and short rests. The timing below is our planning estimate, not an official guided-tour duration.

## Understand the route before leaving the subway

The [official Seoul City Wall Naksan trail page](https://seoulcitywall.seoul.go.kr/en/wallcourse/2.do) currently lists the Hyehwamun-to-Heunginjimun section as 3.2 kilometers and approximately one hour of walking. It identifies Naksan as a low inner-city hill and lists round-the-clock access. Those details describe the trail, not the opening hours of museums, toilets or nearby businesses.

You may find shorter distances in older guides or routes starting partway up the hill. To compare like with like, check the start and finish pins rather than arguing over one distance. Station approaches, a detour to a viewpoint and a meal all add time to the official walking estimate.

For the full direction described here, use Hansung Univ. Station on Line 4 and locate Hyehwamun Gate. The official trail information identifies Exit 4 as the nearby approach. Save both Hyehwamun and Heunginjimun: their English names look similar but refer to different gates. Hyehwa Station is another access option, not the same station as Hansung Univ.

## Hyehwamun to the wall path

Begin by checking the signed pedestrian route. The surviving wall and modern roads do not form one uninterrupted promenade at every junction. Use crossings and public paths rather than cutting through a residential stairway because a navigation app draws a shorter line.

The first part is a good place to settle into your walking pace. If you are already breathing hard, reduce the plan to a short out-and-back while you still know the route. A low hill can feel demanding in humid weather, especially after a long sightseeing morning.

Look at the masonry as well as the skyline. Blocks vary in size, finish and arrangement; the walk becomes more interesting when you notice these differences instead of treating the wall only as a photo background. Observe from the public path without climbing the stones or touching inscriptions.

A useful habit is to photograph a route sign at a junction. That gives you a reference if you later decide to turn back, and it is more reliable than remembering which narrow alley "seemed to go downhill." Keep your phone battery for navigation as well as pictures.

## Naksan Park and sunset timing

Naksan Park is the natural pause in this route. Give yourself time to find a public viewpoint, drink water and decide whether to complete the southern section. The best stop is not necessarily the first crowded railing. Stay on marked paths and allow others to pass when taking photographs.

For sunset, check the actual sunset time for your travel date and arrive with daylight to spare. A planning buffer of 45 to 60 minutes before sunset lets you make decisions while the steps and junctions remain easy to see. This is a buffer, not a prediction of colorful skies; cloud and haze may hide the view.

Do not start the entire route at sunset expecting all its views to be in golden light. If you mainly want a short evening viewpoint, choose a shorter park approach and a known way back. The full gate-to-gate walk works better when most of your navigation happens before dark.

Carry a light layer if the forecast calls for a cooler evening, and footwear with grip. Wet leaves and smooth steps are reasons to shorten the walk. An open trail is not an assurance that every surface is suitable for every visitor in every condition.

## Continue toward Dongdaemun, not every side alley

From the park, continue toward the southern wall section and Heunginjimun. The [Seoul Metropolitan Government's route introduction](https://english.seoul.go.kr/seoul-city-wall-naksan-mountain-trail/) describes the broad north-to-south connection through the Naksan area to Dongdaemun. Use current on-site signs for junctions and any temporary diversions.

Ihwa is a residential neighborhood, not an outdoor set with guaranteed murals. Keep conversation quiet, avoid photographing people inside homes, and do not enter a doorway for a better angle. If a lane has a restriction or a resident asks visitors to move on, return to the public route.

At the southern end, view Heunginjimun from permitted pedestrian areas and use marked crossings around traffic. Dongdaemun Station serves Lines 1 and 4. It is different from Dongdaemun History & Culture Park Station, so check which station your return journey actually uses.

Finish here if you have already had enough walking. Adding shopping, another park and dinner across the city can turn a pleasant two-hour outing into an exhausting evening. A seated meal near your return route is often the more useful final stop.

## Two versions that fit different visitors

For the complete outing, allow 15 to 20 minutes to leave Hansung Univ. Station and orient yourself, roughly 45 to 60 minutes for the first wall section and park pause, then another 40 to 60 minutes to continue toward Dongdaemun with stops. These estimates include a slower sightseeing pace and will vary with your exact route.

For a shorter visit, approach Naksan Park from Hyehwa and return by a familiar public route. You see less of the wall but avoid committing to the full traverse. Do not call the short version universally accessible: the park approaches can still be steep. Ask for current step-free options before traveling with a wheelchair or stroller.

The walk itself does not require buying a guided-tour package. Set money aside for transport, water and a meal; any separately visited attraction has its own admission policy. A cafe purchase is optional, not a requirement to enjoy a public viewpoint.

Keep the day bag small. Bring water before starting rather than expecting a shop at the exact moment you need one. For families, decide on a turnaround point in advance, and let the slowest walker determine whether the southern section still makes sense.

## Pair it with the right part of Seoul

This route belongs in a central or northeastern Seoul day. It is not a quick addition between two appointments on opposite sides of the Han River. If your day already includes a palace and several hours on foot, choose either the wall or another walking district, not both by default.

For a lower-intensity cafe outing, compare our [Ikseon-dong guide](/en/travel/seoul/ikseon-dong-hanok-cafes-walking-route/). If rain makes the hillside unappealing, use the [National Museum of Korea guide](/en/travel/seoul/national-museum-korea-visitor-guide/) to plan a separate indoor visit, allowing for the cross-city journey.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Naksan the same place as Namsan?

No. They are different hills and different outings. N Seoul Tower is on Namsan; this route follows the Naksan section of Seoul City Wall.

### Can I walk here at night?

The official trail page lists 24-hour access, but that does not make every side path or facility suitable after dark. First-time visitors should learn their route in daylight and respect temporary closures.

### Is one hour enough?

It is the official approximate walking duration for the listed section. Add time for station access, rests, photographs and route-finding. About two hours is a more forgiving sightseeing allowance.
